Actor tips · Secrets

19 August 2026 · Raden Films

Character secrets:Something only they know.

A lot of actors invent a private, unscripted fact for the person they are playing — something only that character knows. It never has to hit the page. It adds subtext and specificity without rewriting the dialogue.

These are sparks, not homework you must complete. If the secret starts acting instead of you, drop it. Pair it with proper prep (goal, other people, transitions), not instead of it.

They love someone they should not say it to

Unrequited, or simply unspoken. It colours every interaction: how long they hold an eyeline, what they laugh at, what they cannot bear to hear. Keep it specific — a name, a near-miss — so it is not a generic “yearning” wash.

A habit nobody else sees

A private ritual for comfort or luck: the way they touch a ring, count stairs, never sit with their back to the door. You do not mime it for the camera unless the scene wants it. You know it. That is usually enough.

The fear they will not name

It quietly steers behaviour — who they defer to, when they joke, when they shut down. Name the fear in one concrete image (lifts, hospitals, being left in a car park) so it can actually affect the scene.

Something that still happened

Past trauma is a loaded word. Use it as an event that still shapes how they see the room, not as an excuse to play “damaged” as a flavour. If the sides are light, a heavy invented history can fight the text. Match the size.

A goal they are not advertising

Secret ambition: the job they want, the person they want to replace, the town they want to leave. It sits under the small talk. Useful when the scene is polite and the stakes are not written on the page.

Family they have not mentioned

An unrevealed relationship — sibling in the company, parent who knows the other character, a child the scene never names. It changes status and heat without a speech about it.

The embarrassing thing they like

A guilty pleasure: a show, a snack, a superstition, a crush on a band. Human and specific. It is often more playable than another layer of tragedy.

The ethical knot they keep private

A moral dilemma they have not confessed. They are already choosing, every time they open their mouth. Good for scenes that look like logistics (who signs, who lies to the client) and feel empty without an inner conflict.

Someone who is not in the room

An imaginary friend, a dead parent they still consult, a voice they argue with. Easy to overplay. If you use it, keep it behind the eyes. Casting should feel counsel or comfort, not a second character you are puppeteering.

The thing they would undo

Secret regret: a past choice that still echoes. Let it live in how they hesitate before a similar choice in this scene. Do not announce it with a faraway stare on every line.

Pick one. Do not stack the set.

Ten secrets is a mood board. One that fits the sides is a person. If you are always “the copper” or “the mum in the waiting room,” a secret can stop the type from flattening — it is not a substitute for knowing your casting. Prep the scene first; sprinkle after.
