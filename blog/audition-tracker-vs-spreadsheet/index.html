Audition tracker app vs spreadsheet:When Sheets is enough — and when it isn’t.

Most actors start in Google Sheets. That’s not wrong. The question is when the spreadsheet stops being a tool and starts being another job you avoid.

Here’s a straight comparison — not a dunk on Sheets, not a sales pitch dressed as advice.

When a spreadsheet is enough

Stay in Sheets (or Excel / Numbers) if:

  • You update it the same day as every tape
  • You’re happy keeping sides and videos in Drive or Photos separately
  • You don’t need a lock-screen reminder for due times
  • You’re fine building simple filters for booked / casting office yourself

Plenty of working actors run that way for years. Curt at Acting Career Center has tracked in a log since 2009 and shares a free template. Free Sheet packs from places like Demo Reels for Actors get you columns tonight.

If that describes you, don’t buy an app out of guilt. Open the Sheet. Log the job. Done. More on the habit in how to keep an audition log.

Where spreadsheets usually break

The cracks show up in the same places:

  • Deadlines — a date column isn’t a calendar. Missed self-tapes are often admin failures; see never miss a self-tape deadline.
  • Files — the “final” take lives in Camera Roll; the Sheet has a title and a prayer.
  • Booking rate — doable with formulas, abandoned when you’re tired. See booking rate explained.
  • Phone-first weeks — you’re in a spare room or on a train; the laptop Sheet is at home.
  • Casting contacts — possible in a column, messy when you want a clean list of offices that actually saw you.

None of that means Sheets is “bad.” It means the tool no longer matches the pain.

When an audition tracker app earns it

Consider a dedicated app when the admin is costing you more than the acting prep:

  • Due dates need to hit Calendar / Home without copy-paste
  • Sides and tape should sit on the job
  • You want booking rate and filters without maintaining formulas
  • Casting names should gather into contacts over time
  • You’re on iPhone or iPad for most of the workday

Audition Tracker is built for that loop: add the audition, keep status current, dates show on the calendar, attachments on the card, Stats when you want numbers, Emails for casting contacts. Premium can import from casting email so you’re not retyping the brief. One-time App Store purchase; Premium optional.

Other apps exist too — pick whatever you’ll open. The wrong choice is three half-used systems.

Honest middle ground

Some people keep Sheets as the long archive and an app for live jobs. That’s fine if you don’t double-enter forever. Pick a source of truth for “what’s due this week.” Everything else is backup.

Migrating later is normal. Start where you’ll actually type. Upgrade when the Sheet starts lying to you — usually the week two tapes collide and the file isn’t where the deadline is.

Bottom line

Spreadsheet: cheap, flexible, enough if you maintain it. App: better when deadlines, files, and stats need to live on the phone you already carry.

If you’re ready for the phone version: get Audition Tracker. If not, open a Sheet tonight and log your last five jobs so the page doesn’t feel empty.
