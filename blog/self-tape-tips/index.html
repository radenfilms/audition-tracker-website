Self-tape tips: light, frame, sound — then act

A self-tape is not a mini film set. Casting is watching on a laptop between other tapes. They need to see you, hear you, and believe the scene. Tech is there so the acting can land. That’s the whole job of the kit.

This is the same checklist we keep in Audition Tracker’s Actor Tips (Self Tape Prep): light, background, frame, sound, gear — then let go and play. If the tape looks like a hostage video, they never get to the choices.

You do not need a studio

You need even light on the face, a quiet room, a stable camera at eye level, and a wall that does not steal the shot.

Spend ten minutes on that before you grind take 14. Most “bad tapes” are not bad acting. They are backlight, boom from a phone on a stack of books, and a fridge humming through the take.

Face the light. Always.

Face toward the light. Window, ring light, or a cheap softbox — whatever gives you an even face without raccoon eyes.

  • Avoid sitting with a bright window behind you (you go silhouette).
  • Avoid a single harsh lamp from one side unless that is a choice you can justify in close-up.
  • Check the eyes. If you cannot see them, neither can casting.

Natural light is free and often kinder than a cheap ring light on full blast. If you use a ring, pull it back so you do not get that plastic “influencer” shine unless the role wants it.

Make the wall disappear

Plain light grey, blue, or white. That’s it. Clear clutter, posters, houseplants creeping into frame, and a duvet with a personality.

Wrinkles in a sheet backdrop read as “I filmed this in a panic.” Smooth it. Step a little off the wall so you do not melt into it, but do not stand so far you look like a wide establishing shot of your kitchen.

Chest up unless they say otherwise

Default to a medium close-up — chest up — unless the breakdown says full length, slate in a specific way, or two sizes.

  • Camera at eye level, not looking up your nose or down at a laptop on a coffee table.
  • A little headroom. Do not crop the top of the skull.
  • If they ask for a full-body or a profile, do that take as well. Follow the email, not Instagram.

Picture the scene for the lens: eyelines, where the other person would sit, how big you are in frame. Perform for camera without going “presentational.”

Quiet room first. Mic second.

Close windows. Kill the washing machine. Put the dog in another room (sorry). Speak at a natural, clear volume — not theatre-to-the-back-row, not a mumble into your chest.

An external mic helps once the room is already quiet. A lav or a shotgun into a quiet space will beat the iPhone mic in a tiled bathroom every time. Test a line before you roll the real take. Listen back on headphones, not just the phone speaker.

Phone, tripod, five-second test

A tripod or a stable surface. A phone that films 1080p. That is the starter pack.

  1. Lock the phone. Test a five-second clip for focus, crop, and audio.
  2. Check the breakdown for format, file size, naming, and whether they want slate on a separate take.
  3. Do not discover at 10:52pm that they wanted landscape or a specific codec.

Then record yourself — for self-tapes and for in-person prep. The camera shows what casting will actually see. Watch it once as a reader, not as the person who just suffered the take.

Then play the scene

The footer on those in-app tips is the point: relax and focus on the performance. Tech matters. Authenticity is what lands.

Do the homework (who wrote it, what the character wants, who you are talking to). Try opposite choices. Nail the transitions. Then pick a take that is alive, not the one where the lamp is 3% nicer.

When you are done, put the file on the job — not in a Camera Roll graveyard. In Audition Tracker that lives on the audition card under Self Tape, next to the deadline. The log is how you stop missing the upload window.

Send the alive take

  • Light the face. Kill the backlight.
  • Boring wall. Eye-level camera. Chest-up unless they say otherwise.
  • Quiet room beats a fancy mic in a noisy one.
  • Test once. Film. Watch it back. Attach it to the job.

If you’re still shopping for kit

How should I light a self-tape?

Even light on the face — window or soft source in front of you. No bright window behind you. If the eyes disappear, start over.

What background should I use?

Plain light grey, blue, or white. No clutter. Smooth any sheet.

Do I need a ring light and a lav?

Not on day one. A quiet room, a stable phone, and decent daylight will book you more than shopping. Add kit when the room is already solved.

Should I watch my tapes back?

Yes. Once, as casting. Then stop picking at it and send the alive take before the deadline.
