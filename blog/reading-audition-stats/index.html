How to read your audition stats:Numbers that suggest a next move.

Stats are useless if statuses are lies. Once you’ve logged a few honest months — outcomes updated, formats marked — the charts stop being decoration and start answering small career questions.

This is how to look at them without turning a quiet August into an identity crisis. For the headline percentage alone, see actor booking rate explained.

When you’re ready to look

Wait until you have a real sample — dozens of auditions, not eight. Update Pending jobs before you judge anything. Then pick a window: this year, last six months, or a busy season. Comparing “all time” to last Tuesday is how people invent false stories.

Read the funnel, not one number

Look at three layers together:

  • Auditions — volume and format (self-tape vs in person)
  • Callbacks — is anyone bringing you back?
  • Bookings — did any of it convert?

High volume, no callbacks → materials, type, or submissions may need a look. Callbacks without bookings → different problem (chemistry, availability, or simply not the choice). Bookings without many callbacks → common on smaller roles and some commercials. The shape matters more than a single percentage.

Slice the data

Useful filters once the pile is big enough:

  • Source — agent vs self-submit. Where do bookings actually come from?
  • Format — are you stronger on tape or in the room (or just getting more of one)?
  • Role size / project type — co-star, guest, commercial, theatre. Does the mix match what you want?
  • Month / year — spot seasons, not one bad week

Casting offices that keep appearing belong in your contact picture — see how to track casting directors.

Turn a glance into one decision

Every few months, ask: if this trend held, what would I change? One change. Examples:

  • Self-submits never convert → spend evenings on craft or agent relationship, not more random submissions
  • Always the same type → update the reel or talk type with your rep — more on reading typecast from audition history
  • Callbacks up year on year → you’re doing something right; don’t overhaul out of boredom

Export a simple report when your agent asks for the picture — what to send after a busy month. That’s often more useful than a feelings meeting.

What stats won’t do

They won’t make casting fair. They won’t explain every near-miss. They won’t replace taste, timing, or luck. Use them as weather on the pipeline — same rule as booking rate — not as a verdict on whether you’re “good.”

And if you haven’t logged consistently, fix the audition log first. Stats on garbage input are confident nonsense.

A few quick ones

How often should I check audition stats?

Every few months is plenty. Weekly scoreboard watching usually just feeds anxiety.

What’s the most useful stat?

The pair: callback rate and booking rate, sliced by source or format when you have enough rows.

Can I compare myself to other actors?

Not usefully. Counting rules and markets differ. Compare this year to your last year.
