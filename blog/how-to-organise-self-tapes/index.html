Actor guide

20 August 2026 · Raden Films

How to organise self-tape auditions:So the take you send isn’t lost by Thursday.

Most self-tape chaos isn’t the acting. It’s the sides in Downloads, three “final” takes in Camera Roll, and a deadline you half-remembered from an email you already archived.

Light and sound matter — that’s covered in self-tape tips. This is the boring half: keeping each job findable from the moment the brief lands to the moment you hit upload.

What usually goes wrong

Leaving everything in Photos works for one tape. By tape five you’ve got holiday photos mixed with slate takes, and “the good one” is whoever you guess at 11pm.

Drive or Dropbox folders are better, until every project gets a slightly different name and you can’t remember if it was under the show title or the casting office. Spreadsheets help with deadlines, then fail the second you need the actual file next to the due date.

None of that means you’re disorganised as a person. It means the job produces a lot of small files under time pressure, and memory isn’t a filing system.

Read the brief before you open the sides

When the email arrives, resist jumping straight into the scene. Skim once for the practical stuff: when it’s due, how they want it sent, slate notes, frame size, how many takes, and — if they give one — how to name the file.

Write those down somewhere you’ll see them while you edit. Casting offices each have their own habits. Whatever they ask for is the rule for that tape. If they don’t mention a file name, name it clearly with your name and the role so they can find you in a pile — then keep a second copy for yourself with the date on it so you can find it later.

Only then open the sides and do the creative work.

Give every tape one home

Pick one place for active jobs. Year, then project, then role is plenty:

Put the sides in there. Put the brief notes in there. When you export, put the file you actually submit in there. Raw takes can live in a subfolder you delete once you’re done — they’re rarely worth keeping.

Don’t leave the “real” version sitting only in Messages, WhatsApp, or an unsent email draft. Those disappear the week you need them.

On the day

Create the job record (or folder) before you set up the lights. Due date first. Then shoot. Keep the edit simple: slate if they asked for one, scene, hard cuts. No music unless they want it.

Two or three takes is usually enough. Watch the one you like once, the way casting will — on a bigger screen if you can — then send it. Don’t polish the lamp while the deadline ticks.

After you upload, mark it submitted somewhere you’ll trust next week. Attach or save the exact file you sent, not “roughly the same take.”

Folders are fine for long-term storage. They’re awkward when you’re juggling two dues tonight and your agent asks what happened to last month’s crime tape.

That’s enough system

One home for files. Brief notes before sides. Casting’s instructions on the upload. The submitted take living with the job, not only in Photos.
